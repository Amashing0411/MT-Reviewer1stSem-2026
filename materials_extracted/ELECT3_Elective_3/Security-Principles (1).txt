Principles of Security

--- PAGE 2 ---
Confidentiality - The principle of keeping data secret and protecting it from 
being viewed by unauthorized individuals.
Integrity - The principle of ensuring data is accurate, complete, and has not been 
improperly modified or destroyed.
Availability - The principle of ensuring that authorized users can access 
systems and data whenever they are needed.
Authentication - The process of verifying the identity of a user or system (e.g., 
checking a password or biometric).
Authorization - The process of granting specific permissions or access rights 
to an authenticated user for a resource.
Accountability / Non-repudiation - Ensuring that all actions taken within a 
system can be traced back to a specific entity, making denial impossible.
Privacy - The right of individuals to control how their personal and sensitive 
information is collected, used, and shared.

--- PAGE 3 ---
Access Control Models : These are different rule sets for how 
Authorization (what you're allowed to do) is enforced in a system
DAC (Discretionary Access Control) - The owner of a resource (like 
a file) gets to decide who can access it and what permissions they have.
MAC (Mandatory Access Control) - Access is based on a fixed 
security classification level (e.g., Top Secret, Confidential) assigned by a 
central authority.
RBAC (Role-Based Access Control) - Access permissions are 
grouped by the job function or role of the user (e.g., "Accountant," "HR 
Manager"), not the individual.
ABAC (Attribute-Based Access Control) - Access is granted 
based on a combination of many characteristics (attributes) about the 
user, the resource, and the environment (e.g., "Allow access if user is 
HR and the time is 9 am to 5 pm").
Zero Trust - A security model that assumes no user or device, 
inside or outside the network, is trusted by default; every access attempt 
must be verified.


--- PAGE 4 ---
Security Domains
Information Security Management - The high-level process of developing, implementing, and 
maintaining an organization's overall security strategy and framework.
Network Security - The measures taken to protect the organization's network infrastructure 
(wires, routers, Wi-Fi) from unauthorized access or misuse.
Application Security - The security controls built into software applications throughout their 
development to protect them from code-level vulnerabilities.
Cloud Security - The practices used to secure data, applications, and infrastructure hosted with third-
party cloud providers (e.g., AWS, Azure).
Database Security - The defensive measures focused on protecting the structured data stores 
(databases) from unauthorized access, corruption, or theft.
Endpoint Security - The practice of securing end-user devices, such as laptops, desktops, and 
mobile phones, that connect to the network.

--- PAGE 5 ---
Physical Security - The protection of an organization's 
tangible assets (buildings, hardware, people) from real-world 
threats like theft or damage.
IoT Security - The practices dedicated to securing small, 
often resource-constrained Internet of Things (IoT) devices like 
smart cameras and sensors.
Mobile Security - The security measures and 
management policies specifically aimed at protecting data on 
smartphones and tablets.
Cybersecurity Operations - The daily operational 
activities (monitoring, detection, and response) performed to 
maintain the ongoing security of systems.


--- PAGE 6 ---
Policies, Standards & Frameworks
Security Policies & Procedures - Policies are high-level rules defining what must be done; 
Procedures are the detailed, step-by-step instructions for how to do it.
Risk Management & Assessment   - The structured process of identifying, analyzing, and evaluating 
risks, and then deciding on appropriate methods to mitigate or manage them.
Security Controls - Mechanisms (technical or administrative) implemented to prevent, detect, or 
correct security violations and reduce risk.
Compliance & Regulatory Requirements - Adherence to mandatory laws, regulations, or industry 
standards (like GDPR, HIPAA, or PCI-DSS) governing data security.
Incident Response & Recovery - The organized plan and process for identifying, managing, and 
resolving a security breach or attack to minimize damage.
Business Continuity & Disaster Recovery (BCDR) - BC keeps core business functions running 
during a crisis; DR focuses on fully restoring IT systems after a major disaster.

--- PAGE 7 ---
Security Practices
Vulnerability Assessment - The practice of scanning systems to find weaknesses or flaws (vulnerabilities) that a threat 
could exploit.
Penetration Testing (Pen Test) - A method where authorized security experts simulate a real cyberattack against a 
system to find and exploit weaknesses.
Security Auditing & Monitoring- The continuous practice of reviewing logs, activity, and configurations to ensure compliance 
with policies and detect suspicious events.
Patch Management - The systematic process of acquiring, testing, and applying software updates (patches) to fix security 
vulnerabilities.
Intrusion Detection & Prevention (IDS/IPS) - IDS is a system that alerts when a potential attack is happening; IPS is 
a system that automatically blocks the attack in real-time.
Security Awareness Training The ongoing educational process used to teach employees about security risks, policies, and 
how to behave safely to protect company assets.
