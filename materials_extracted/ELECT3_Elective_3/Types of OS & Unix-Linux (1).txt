--- PAGE 2 ---
Single-user OS - supports only one user at a time. No multiple 
accounts, no concurrent users.
Multi-user OS - allows multiple users to use the computer 
simultaneously or remotely. Uses time-sharing, permissions, and 
user isolation.
Multitasking OS - can run multiple tasks/programs simultaneously. 
Uses scheduling and CPU time-slicing.
Distributed OS - manages a group of independent computers and 
makes them appear as one single system. Resources are shared 
across multiple machines in a network.
Real-time OS - an OS designed to process data and respond within 
a strict time constraint.Predictability is more important than raw 
speed.

Comparison of Unix 
and Linux


--- PAGE 4 ---
Unix
• Developed in 1969 at AT&T’s Bell Labs by Ken Thompson & Dennis Ritchie.
• Proprietary (owned by companies like IBM, HP, Oracle).
Linux
• Created in 1991 by Linus Torvalds as a free alternative to Unix.
• Open-source and community-driven.

--- PAGE 5 ---
Usage
Unix: Mostly used in big enterprises, research, banking, and 
telecom systems.
Linux: Used everywhere — from servers, desktops, Android 
phones, supercomputers, IoT devices.
Hardware Support
Unix: Designed for specific hardware (IBM, HP machines).
Linux: Runs on almost any hardware (PCs, laptops, Raspberry Pi, 
supercomputers).
