I. COMMUNICATION
1. Communication: The Lifeline of Organizational
Success
Description:
Communication is the process of sharing information,
ideas, opinions, instructions, and feelings between
people. In an organization, communication is considered
a lifeline because it connects employees, managers,
departments, and leaders. Without proper
communication, employees may misunderstand
instructions, make mistakes, or have difficulty working
together.
Importance of Communication in an Organization:
● Helps employees understand their roles and
responsibilities.
● Allows managers to give clear instructions.
● Encourages teamwork and cooperation.
● Helps solve problems and conflicts.
● Supports decision-making.
● Builds trust between employees and
management.
● Helps the organization achieve its goals.
2. Why Communication is the Lifeline of Business
Description:
Communication is essential in business because almost
every business activity involves exchanging information.
Employees communicate with managers, coworkers,
customers, suppliers, and business partners. Effective
communication helps ensure that the right information
reaches the right person at the right time.
Why it is important:
● Helps businesses communicate their goals.
● Improves employee performance.
● Provides better customer service.
● Helps prevent misunderstandings.
● Supports teamwork between departments.
● Helps businesses make better decisions.
● Builds strong relationships with customers and
employees.
3. Types of Communication
Communication can take different forms depending on
the message, situation, and people involved. Choosing
the appropriate type of communication helps make the
message more effective.
Verbal Communication- Verbal communication
involves using spoken words to communicate
information. It is commonly used when immediate
interaction or clarification is needed.
 Nonverbal Communication- Nonverbal
communication involves sending messages without
directly using words. Body language can
sometimes communicate emotions or attitudes more
strongly than spoken words.
Written Communication- Written communication
uses written words to provide information. It is useful
when information needs to be documented or referred to
later.
Visual Communication- Visual communication uses
images, symbols, charts, graphs, and other visual
elements to communicate information. It can make
complicated information easier to understand.
Formal Communication- Formal communication
follows the official structure and procedures of an
organization. It is usually related to work responsibilities
and organizational activities.
Informal Communication- Informal communication
happens naturally between people and does not
necessarily follow the organization's official
communication structure.
II. COMMUNICATION BASICS
4. Communication Basics and Importance
Communication Process
Sender → Message → Channel → Receiver →
Feedback
1.Sender- The sender is the person who creates and
delivers the message.
2. Message- The message is the information, idea,
instruction, or feeling being communicated.
3. Channel- The channel is the method used to
communicate the message.
4. Receiver- The receiver is the person or group who
receives and interprets the message.
5. Feedback- Feedback is the receiver's response to the
message. It helps the sender know whether the message
was understood.

6. Noise/Barrier- Noise refers to anything that interferes
with communication.
III. WHAT IS COMMUNICATION?
Communication is the process of exchanging
information, ideas, thoughts, opinions, and feelings
between two or more people. It can happen through
speaking, writing, body language, or visual methods.
The main purpose of communication is to create
understanding between the sender and receiver.
IV. EFFECTIVE COMMUNICATION
6. The 7 Cs of Communication
The 7 Cs are important principles that help make
communication effective.
1. Clear- The message should be easy to understand.
2. Concise- The message should be direct and avoid
unnecessary information.
3. Concrete- The message should be specific and
supported by facts or examples.
4. Correct- The information should be accurate and free
from unnecessary errors.
5. Coherent- Ideas should be organized and connected
logically.
6. Complete- The message should contain all important
information needed by the receiver.
7. Courteous- Communication should be respectful,
polite, and considerate.
V. IMPROVING COMMUNICATION SKILLS
7. How to Improve Communication Skills
Ways to Improve:
1. Listen carefully- Give your full attention instead of
preparing your response while the other person is
speaking.
2. Speak clearly- Use words that are appropriate and
easy to understand.
3. Think before speaking- Organize your thoughts
before delivering your message.
4. Use appropriate body language- Maintain eye
contact and use confident but natural gestures.
5. Ask questions- Questions help clarify information
and prevent misunderstandings.
6. Practice empathy- Try to understand the other
person's feelings and point of view.
7. Control your tone- The same words can have
different meanings depending on how they are said.
8. Accept feedback- Feedback helps identify areas
where your communication can improve.
VI. LISTENING SKILLS
8. Listening Skills
Description:
Listening is an active process that involves hearing,
understanding, interpreting, and responding to
information. Unlike hearing, listening requires
concentration and effort.
Hearing vs. Listening
Hearing
● Physical process.
● Happens naturally.
● Involves receiving sounds.
Listening
● Mental and active process.
● Requires attention.
● Involves understanding the message.
VII. GREAT LISTENERS
9. Four Things All Great Listeners Know
1. They Pay Attention- They focus on the speaker and
avoid distractions.
2. They Don't Interrupt- They allow the speaker to
finish expressing their thoughts.
3. They Ask Questions- They ask relevant questions
when they need clarification.
4. They Respond Appropriately- They provide verbal
or nonverbal feedback to show that they understand.

X. BARRIERS TO EFFECTIVE LISTENING
12. Barriers of Effective Listening
Common Barriers:
1. Physical Distractions- Noise, uncomfortable
environments, or poor surroundings.
2. Lack of Attention- The listener is distracted or
thinking about something else.
3. Bias and Prejudice- Judging the speaker or message
before hearing everything.
4. Emotional State- Anger, stress, sadness, or anxiety
can affect concentration.
5. Interrupting- Interrupting prevents the speaker from
completely expressing their thoughts.
6. Selective Listening- Only paying attention to
information that interests you.
7. Assumptions- Assuming you already know what the
speaker will say.
8. Information Overload- Receiving too much
information at one time..

XII. JOB INTERVIEW
14. "Tell Me About Yourself"
Description:
"Tell me about yourself" is often one of the first
questions asked during an interview. The interviewer is
not asking for your entire personal history. They want a
short professional introduction that explains who you
are and why you are qualified for the position.
Recommended Structure:
Present → Past → Future
Present
Explain who you are currently.
Past
Mention relevant education, experience, skills, or
achievements.
Future
Explain your career goals or why you are interested in
the position.
Example:

--- PAGE 4 ---
"I am a Computer Engineering student
with experience in technology-related
projects and student organizations. These
experiences helped me develop my
communication, teamwork, leadership, and
problem-solving skills. I am interested in
this position because I want to apply what I
have learned while gaining more
professional experience."
Remember:
Keep your answer relevant, confident, and concise.
XIII. INTERVIEW QUESTION: WEAKNESSES
15. "What Are Your Weaknesses?"
Description:
This question tests your self-awareness and
willingness to improve. Employers want to know
whether you recognize areas that need improvement and
whether you are taking action to develop yourself.
Good Answer Structure:
Weakness → Action → Improvement
Example:
"One of my weaknesses is that I sometimes
spend too much time checking my work
because I want to make sure everything is
accurate. To improve this, I have been
learning to manage my time better and set
specific deadlines for each task."
Avoid Answers Like:
"I don't have any weaknesses."
Giving a weakness without explaining how you are
improving.
Mentioning a serious weakness that directly prevents
you from performing the job.
Best Approach:
Choose a genuine but manageable weakness and show
that you are actively working to improve it.
XIV. INTERVIEW PREPARATION
16. How to Prepare for an Interview
Description:
Interview preparation helps you become more confident
and organized. Preparing beforehand allows you to
answer questions more naturally instead of thinking of
everything for the first time during the actual interview.
Before the Interview:
● Research the company.
● Understand the job description.
● Review your resume.
● Practice common interview questions.
● Prepare examples of your skills and experiences.
● Prepare questions for the interviewer.
● Choose appropriate professional attire.
● Prepare necessary documents.
During the Interview:
● Arrive on time.
● Greet the interviewer professionally.
● Maintain appropriate eye contact.
● Listen carefully.
● Answer questions directly.
● Speak clearly and confidently.
● Avoid interrupting.
● Be honest.
● Show enthusiasm for the position.
After the Interview:
● Thank the interviewer.
● Review your performance.
● Identify areas you can improve.
● Follow up professionally when appropriate.
