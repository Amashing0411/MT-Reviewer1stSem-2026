SYSTEM DEFINITION & DEPLOYMENT
Requirements Traceability & Conversion
Requirements Traceability Chain System Conversion Strategies
User Requirement: Vague operational need ("Call help after 
shower fall").
System Requirement: Functional target ("Detect sudden 
impact; resist water").
System Specification: Measurable criteria ("3-axis MEMS 
>2.5G; IP68 casing").
Traceability Matrix: Maps every technical spec to user 
needs.
Parallel Installation: Old and new run together; low risk, high 
cost.
Pilot Operation: Small user cohort rollout; moderate risk and 
cost.
Cold Turkey (Big Bang): Immediate cutover; low cost, 
extreme failure risk.
Postmortem Review: Captures lessons learned before team 
disbands.

PLAN-DRIVEN SDLC MODEL (1 OF 5)
Waterfall Model: Linear & Sequential
Figure 2: Sequential Waterfall Phase Progression
Sequential Phase-Gate Architecture
Overview: Linear stages where each phase freezes before next 
starts.
Proposed By: Winston W. Royce (1970); Herbert D. Benington 
(1956).
Advantages: Clear milestones, disciplined documentation, simple 
cost tracking.
Disadvantages: Rigid structure; prohibitively expensive late 
requirement changes.
Best-Case Scenario: Fixed, well-understood requirements and 
stable technology.
Worst-Case Scenario: Exploratory R&D or volatile user-facing 
software.

--- PAGE 6 ---
Overview Pairs every design stage with a matching test phase.
Proposed By Paul Rook (1986) & German Federal V-Modell.
Advantages Strict bidirectional traceability; early defect detection.
Disadvantages Inflexible to mid-project changes; heavy documentation.
Best-Case Scenario ISO 26262 automotive, avionics, and medical devices.
Worst-Case Scenario Consumer startups with rapidly shifting scope.

RISK-DRIVEN EVOLUTIONARY SDLC (3 OF 5)
Spiral Model: Iterative Risk Analysis
Figure 4: Boehm's Four-Quadrant Risk Spiral
Risk-First Cyclic Progression
Overview: Combines iterative prototyping with systematic 
Waterfall control.
Proposed By: Barry Boehm (1986) in IEEE Computer.
Advantages: Continuous risk mitigation; handles evolving technical 
uncertainty.
Disadvantages: Expensive administration; requires specialized risk-
analysis expertise.
Best-Case Scenario: Large-scale, high-budget aerospace, defense, 
or enterprise systems.
Worst-Case Scenario: Small, low-budget, low-risk projects with 
simple scope.

--- PAGE 8 ---
Overview Builds quick interactive models to solidify user requirements.
Proposed By Fred Brooks (1975); Naumann & Jenkins (1982); Martin (1991).
Advantages Resolves vague requirements early; prevents costly misbuilds.
Disadvantages Users mistake throwaway prototypes for finished production code.
Best-Case Scenario UI/UX-heavy apps where users struggle with written specs.
Worst-Case Scenario Purely algorithmic backends or shipping unscalable prototype code.

--- PAGE 9 ---
ADAPTIVE INCREMENTAL SDLC (5 OF 5)
Agile Scrum: Iterative Sprints
Figure 6: Agile Scrum Backlog, Sprints & Increments
Incremental Time-Boxed Delivery
Overview: Delivers working software increments in 1–4 week
sprints.
Proposed By: Takeuchi & Nonaka (1986); Schwaber & Sutherland 
(1995).
Advantages: High adaptability; rapid customer feedback; 
continuous value delivery.
Disadvantages: Scope drift risk; harder long-term cost and 
schedule prediction.
Best-Case Scenario: Dynamic commercial SaaS and evolving digital 
products.
Worst-Case Scenario: Fixed-price contracts or standalone 
physical hardware builds.
